Electrons are negatively charged subatomic particles held around the atomic nucleus through their attraction to the positively charged protons. They are what determines the chemical properties of an atom, setting the electrical charge and storing the potential energy. The amount of potential energy stored in an atom is determined by the organization of electrons in different energy levels of the cloud, corresponding to distance from the nucleus (more distance = more potential energy).

Electrons can occupy different shells (energy levels) depending on how many there are. Each shell has a characteristic average distance from the nucleus, energy level, and capacity. The chemical behavior of the atom is primarily determined by the electrons in the outermost shell, called the **valence shell**, and the electrons occupying it called **valence electrons**. Atoms with the same number of electrons in their valence shells exhibit similar chemical behavior. Generally, the more full the valence shell, the less reactive the atom - atoms with full valence shells are inert and unreactive.

In reality electrons do not exist in concentric circles around the nucleus, instead existing in orbitals, which have different shapes and amounts depending on the energy level (shell). The reactivity of an atom depends on its unpaired electrons in one or more orbitals of its valence shell. Different atoms interact in ways that complete each others valence shells, pairing unpaired electrons in different types of chemical bonds.
