**Lysosomes** are membrane bound sacs of *hydrolytic* enzymes that are used to digest (hydrolyze) [[Polymers and Macromolecules|macromolecules]] into smaller subunits suitable for cellular use. The hydrolytic enzymes work most efficiently in an [[Acidity|acidic]] environment, which is sustained within the lysosomal membrane and kept separated from the more neutral environment found within the cytosol.  Lysosomes perform digestion in a variety of ways. One of which is by fusing with a food vacuole or smaller organism which was engulfed via **phagocytosis** and mixing with and breaking down its contents. The digestive products such as simple [[Carbohydrates|sugars]], [[Proteins#Amino Acids|amino acids]], and other monomers then pass into the cytosol for use as nutrients within the cell. Lysosomes are also responsible for recycling the cells own organic material through a process called **autophagy**. In this process, a damaged organelle or volume of cytosol is surrounded by a double membrane effectively forming a new vesicle which the lysosome fuses to via the outer membrane, allowing its enzymes to dissolve the inner membrane and its contents.
