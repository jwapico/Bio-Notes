DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is a large [[Nucleic Acids|nucleic acid]] consisting of a sequence of nucleotides strung together that encode all of the proteins and regulatory mechanisms that define an organism. 

It is located in the [[Nucleus|nucleus]] of eukaryotic cells and the cytoplasm of prokaryotic cells in the form of **chromosomes**. Each chromosome contains hundreds or thousands of genes, each of which a sub-sequence in the DNA. A singular gene contains the information necessary to build a molecular structure; a cells functionality and identity are determined by all of its genes.

This information is encoded within DNAs molecular structure. DNA is made up of two long strands of nucleotides arranged in a double helix. There are 4 types of nucleotides, A, T, C, and G. It is the ordering of these nucleotides that encodes the information. 
- The protein structure coding section of the DNA is divided into 3 letter sequences called [[Codons|codons]].

DNA is expressed indirectly through **transcription** into RNA (mRNA), which is **translated** by [[Ribosomes|ribosomes]] into a chain of amino acids which folds to create the 3 dimensional structure of the polypeptide, which then may bond with other polypeptides to form the final [[Proteins|protein]]. This entire process is known as **gene expression**.

The entire set of genetic instructions that an organism inherits is called its **genome**, and the entire set of proteins expressed by a given cell, tissue, or organism is a **proteome**. Likewise, the act of studying sets of genes is **genomics**, and sets of proteins, **proteomics**.

> This process, the organelles involved, and the ways in which the resulting proteins are transported for use within the cell are outlined in [[Endomembrane System|the endomembrane system.]]
