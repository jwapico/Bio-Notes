DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is a sequence of nucleotides that encodes all the proteins and molecular structures that make up an organism. 

It is located in the nucleus of eukaryotic cells and the cytoplasm of prokaryotic cells in the form of chromosomes. Each chromosome contains hundreds or thousands of genes, each of which a sub-sequence in the DNA. A singular gene contains the information necessary to build a molecular structure, and a cells function and identity are determined by all of its genes.

The way in which this information is encoded is within DNAs molecular structure. It is made up two long chains of nucleotides called strands, arranged in a double helix. There are 4 types of nucleotides, A, T, C, and G. It is the ordering and specific sequences of these nucleotides that encodes the molecular structure. 
- The protein structure coding section of the DNA is divided into 3 letter sequences called [[Codons]].

Proteins are encoded (transcribed) indirectly through mRNA, which is then translated into a chain of amino acids, which fold to create the 3 dimensional structure of the final protein. This entire process is known as gene expression.

The entire set of genetic instructions that an organism inherits is called its **genome**, and the entire set of proteins expressed by a given cell, tissue, or organism is a **proteome**. Likewise, the act of studying sets of genes is **genomics**, and sets of proteins, **proteomics**.
