**Organic chemistry** is the study of the structure and molecular functions of molecules which are built upon **carbon** [[Fundamentals of Matter|atoms]]. Carbon is the backbone to these molecular structures because it has 6 [[Electrons|electrons]], 2 filing the inner shell and 4 in the valence shell, allowing it to [[Chemical Bonds#Covalent Bonds|covalently bond]] with up to 4 other atoms. Carbon most frequently bonds with hydrogen, oxygen, and nitrogen, these 4 atoms (& sulfur and phosphorus) act as the main building blocks for all possible organic molecules. Each of which has properties which, when leveraged by evolution, serve as the foundation for life.

**Hydrocarbons** are molecules that consist of long chains of only carbon and hydrogen. They store a lot of energy in these covalent bonds, and can undergo reactions that release a large amount of energy. This is why many organisms build them to store as energy. The structure of many of them also forms in such a way that there are large unpolar areas on the molecule, making them hydrophobic, this is why fatty oils don't mix with water. 

![[Pasted image 20251109225211.png]]

These carbon skeletons can combine to form larger and larger 3 dimensional structures. Compounds that have the same numbers of atoms and elements can have different structures, these compounds are called **isomers.** 

![[Pasted image 20251109225346.png]]

These carbon skeletons can also have different chemical groups attached to different parts of the compound. The chemical groups alter the molecular shape, contributing to it's function. Chemical groups can also be involved in chemical reactions, these groups are known as **functional groups**.

![[Pasted image 20251109230849.png]]

Chemical groups themselves can also form structures, such as in ATP, adenosine triphosphate.

![[Pasted image 20251109230943.png]]

When three phosphate groups are present in series like this, one of them can react with water in a reaction that yields energy and HOPO3^2- transforming the starting molecule (ATP) into ADP. This molecule is a critical carrier of potential energy in cells, and is the leading driver behind cellular work.
