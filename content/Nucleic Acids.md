**Nucleic acids** are large [[Polymers and Macromolecules|polymers]] called **polynucleotides** made of individual **nucleotides.**  A nucleotide is made of a 5 carbon sugar skeleton (pentose), a nitrogenous base encoding genetic information, and phosphate groups [[Chemical Bonds#Covalent Bonds|covalently bonding]] with the sugar(s).

There are two classes of nitrogenous bases, *pyrimidines* and *purines.* **Pyrimidines** consist of a single 6-membered ring with carbon and nitrogen, while **purines** have *two* rings. There are 3 bases in the pyrimidine family, cytosine (C), thymine (T), and uracil (U), while there are 2 purines, adenine (A) and guanine (G). Both purines and cytosine are found in DNA *and* RNA, thymine is only found in DNA, and uracil is only found in RNA.

The encoding bases are strung together through phosphodiester linkages formed via [[Polymers and Macromolecules|condensation reactions]] in the phosphate groups connecting the sugars, forming the *sugar-phosphate backbone*. The ends of the polymer are distinct from each other, the 5' end has a phosphate attached to the 5' carbon, while the 3' end has a hydroxyl group on the 3' carbon. These distinct ends create a directionality inherent to the structure utilized by life to store genetic information in the 5' to 3' direction.

Certain bases are chemically compatible with each other, allowing them to share a [[Chemical Bonds#Hydrogen Bonds|hydrogen bond]]; adenine always pairs with thymine, and guanine always pairs with cytosine. This means that two **complementary** strands of bases can be linked together in antiparallel fashion, forming the double helix structure of DNA. It is this complementary property that allows DNA to be copied, preserving its information.

RNA on the other hand exists as single strands, though complementary base pairing can still occur at regions of other RNA molecules, and regions on the *same* strand. This allows RNA to take various 3 dimensional forms, which can have distinct and versatile functional uses. In RNA adenine pairs with uracil.
