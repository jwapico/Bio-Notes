**Vacuoles** are effectively large vesicles with selective membranes that control the contents of the containing solution, separating it from the cytosol. This functionality lends to a variety of functional uses within a cell. One type of vacuole is the *food vacuole*, which contains cellular nutrients extracted via the lysosomes. Unicellular protists have *contractile vacuoles* which allow it to pump excess water out of the cell and maintain internal ionic and molecular concentrations. Plant and fungal vacuoles exhibit particularly diverse functionality, carrying out enzymatic hydrolysis (similar to that seen in [[Lysosomes|lysosomes]]), storing organic compounds, poisonous or unpalatable compounds serving to protect against predators, and pigments that can attract pollinators. Additionally, plant cells generally contain a large **central [[Vacuoles|vacuole]]**, which stores 'cell sap', the cells main repository for inorganic compounds such as potassium and chloride. This central vacuole also plays an important role in plant growth, enlarging as the cell uptakes more water, physically increasing its size with minimal investment in new cytoplasm.
