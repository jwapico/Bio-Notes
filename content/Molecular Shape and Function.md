A molecule has a characteristic size and shape, which determines its function in molecular biology. These shapes are determined by the amount and type of [[Chemical Bonds|chemical bonds]], which are informed by the structure of [[Electrons|electrons]]. For O2 or H2 for example, the molecular structure is linear since there are only two atoms, but if you add more atoms the arrangements of atoms and electrical charges gets much more complicated.

Building blocks and patterns emerge from these arrangements, forming larger structures that can be utilized and built by life and evolution.

A molecules behavior in and around water is fundamental to its function within biology. This property is determined by the [[Chemical Bonds|bonds]] it makes with the water molecules near it. A substance is **hydrophilic** if it is attracted to water, and **hydrophobic** if it is not. Hydrophilic substances tend to have very polar regions, attracting water with hydrogen bonds and [[Electrons|electron]] imbalances, while hydrophobic substances are non polar and electrons in the molecule are spread equally and symmetrically.
