Many of the transport vesicles produced by the ER are received by the **Golgi apparatus**, which further modifies, tags, and repackages the products for transport within the cell. Physically, the Golgi consists of a group of associated, flat membranous sacs (**cisternae**), which have a structural directionality, with membranes of cisternae in different regions exhibiting distinct structure and molecular composition. The two sides are referred to as the *cis* and *trans* face, acting as the shipping and receiving areas, respectively. The *cis* face is logically located near the ER, where vesicles are transporting materials to be fused with the Golgi for further processing. On the other side, the *trans* face forms and pinches off new vesicles containing the modified, final form of the protein or macromolecule to be transported somewhere else within the cell. As the molecular products traverse the Golgi from the *cis* to *trans* side, they undergo many transformations finalizing their form. Different parts of the Golgi have distinct specialized enzymes which transform the products as they progress through the structure, making the Golgi somewhat analogous to a manufacturing line. The Golgi also sorts and tags the vesicles for transport to specific parts of the cell by adding molecular identification tags such as phosphate groups or binding sites. Many of the transport vesicles released by the Golgi are eventually incorporated into the plasma membrane, increasing its size and dispersing the rest of vesicles contents into the cytosol.
